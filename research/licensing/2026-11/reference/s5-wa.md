## Western Australia

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Authority and contact | Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions (DBCA), Wildlife Licensing Section. Phone (08) 9219 9831, or (08) 9219 9000 and choose Wildlife Licensing. Email wildlifelicensing@dbca.wa.gov.au. Post: Locked Bag 30, Bentley Delivery Centre WA 6983. Web: [Fauna licences](https://www.dbca.wa.gov.au/node/9635). |
| Official keepable-species list | Appendix 1 of the [CEO Licensing Guideline: Fauna possessing (pet keeper's) licence](https://dbca.wa.gov.au/media/772/download) (30 Dec 2018; web page last updated 12 Apr 2023), with the species list at [media/771](https://dbca.wa.gov.au/media/771/download). It is a positive list: only listed species can be licensed. |
| Licence names and what each allows | **Fauna possessing (pet keeper's) licence**, designated **standard** or **advanced** (Biodiversity Conservation Regulations 2018, r 36). A standard licence needs a suitable secure enclosure. An advanced licence also needs a locked enclosure, written references, and evidence you hold or have held a standard licence. The guideline asks all applicants to show 12 months' compliance with licence conditions. The licence may limit who you can transfer animals to (other pet keeper's licence holders, pet dealers, vets). Licences run for up to 3 years (r 80). |
| Exempt species | No reptiles or frogs. The no-licence pets on the list are birds (from the Nov 2026 extraction of Appendix 1). |
| Minimum age and guardian rules | Over 18 (guideline 5.1.3). |
| Cost (fee year) | Fees apply to applications, renewals and licences, and an inspection fee may apply. Amounts are **unconfirmed**: the fees schedule in the regulations could not be reached. |
| Processing time | **Unconfirmed.** Renewal applications should go in at least 20 business days before expiry. |
| Record keeping and returns | Set by licence conditions (regulations 85–86). The specific requirements are **unconfirmed**. |
| Buying, selling and moving within the state | Set by licence conditions. Transfers may be limited to other licence holders, dealers or vets. Bringing native fauna into WA needs a DBCA import licence, even if the animal is licensed in your home state. Eastern-states species not on the WA list can't be brought in. ([Bringing plants and animals into WA](https://dbca.wa.gov.au/licences-and-permits/fauna/bringing-plants-and-animals-western-australia)) |
| Recent or upcoming changes | DBCA reviewed the species list. Public expressions of interest ran from 8 Sep to 6 Oct 2025 and are now closed ([DBCA news](https://www.dbca.wa.gov.au/news/2025/public-feedback-sought-part-pet-keepers-licence-review); [review page](https://www.dbca.wa.gov.au/node/12264)). A panel will recommend additions, removals and moves between tiers. **No outcome or new list had been published as of 8 Nov 2026.** Pythons, crocodiles and marine species were outside the review. |
| Venomous species | Some, such as Tiger Snake, Dugite, Mulga Snake and Southern Death Adder, are on the advanced list and marked "dangerous". The dangerous marking also covers some non-venomous species, such as Olive Python and Gould's Monitor. Dangerous species need an advanced licence and a pre-approval inspection: lockable enclosures, a labelled room and safety equipment. |
| Last checked | 8 Nov 2026 |

> **Authority's internal categories (for Rowan only, never app copy):** Category A (no licence), Category B (standard) and Category C (advanced). The older regulations used "Category 4" for the former standard tier.
