## Australian Capital Territory

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Authority and contact | Conservator of Flora and Fauna. Licences are run by the Nature Conservation Licensing and Compliance Team, Environment and Specialised Regulation, Access Canberra. Phone 13 22 81 (Access Canberra). The page gives no email address. Web: [Keeping or selling animals in the ACT](https://www.act.gov.au/environment/animals-and-plants/licensing-of-plants-and-animals/keeping-or-selling-animals-in-the-act). |
| Official keepable-species list | There is no positive list. Each application is assessed against the [Conservator Guidelines 2024 (NI2024-486)](https://legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/ni/2024-486/current/PDF/2024-486.PDF), which also name the animals that won't be licensed. Exempt animals are in the Nature Conservation (Exempt Animals) Declaration 2026 (DI2026-61), in force from 15 May 2026. |
| Licence names and what each allows | **Licence to keep** a non-exempt animal: a nature conservation licence, valid for 3 years and assessed case by case. The Conservator weighs the animal's conservation status, captive-bred source, welfare needs, enclosure, danger, escape risk and your experience. You need the licence before you acquire the animal, and adding a species means a new application. **Licence to sell** is separate: it runs one year, your licence number must appear in ads, and you can sell only to licence holders. **Import** and **export** licences are needed for each movement. |
| Exempt species | **No reptiles since 15 May 2026.** The 2026 declaration removed the Eastern Blue-tongue, Blotched Blue-tongue, Shingleback, Eastern Bearded Dragon and Eastern Long-necked Turtle. Frogs: only tadpoles and juveniles of the Brown-striped (Striped Marsh) Frog, Eastern Banjo Frog and Spotted Grass Frog are exempt. Adults need a licence. |
| Minimum age and guardian rules | Not stated. |
| Cost (fee year) | 2026–27, from 1 Jul 2026 ([Fees Determination DI2026-133](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/di/2026-133/current/PDF/2026-133.PDF)). Keep fewer than 20 non-exempt animals: $21.96. Keep 20 or more: $56.07. Sell up to 10 a year: $32.88 per year (11–100: $57.29; over 100: $351.77). Non-commercial import or export: nil. Adding a species: no extra fee. |
| Processing time | 28 days from submission. |
| Record keeping and returns | Record every non-exempt animal in the Nature Conservation Regulation Record Book and produce it when asked. Notify a change of address within 30 days. Keep animals only at the licensed location. Conservation Officers inspect regularly. The Animal Welfare (Private Keeping of Native Reptiles) Mandatory Code of Practice 2023 also applies. |
| Buying, selling and moving within the state | Buy only from people licensed to sell. Selling needs a sale licence. Interstate imports need an import licence (non-commercial licences last up to 3 months). |
| Recent or upcoming changes | 15 May 2026: the 2019 exempt declaration (DI2019-66) was repealed and replaced by DI2026-61, so five reptiles lost their exemption. A press report says fees were waived for existing keepers who applied by 14 Aug 2026; this is **unconfirmed** on official pages, and the window has closed anyway. Fees rose on 1 Jul 2026. |
| Venomous species | **Not permitted for private keepers.** Licences won't be issued to keep venomous snakes, except to zoos, rehabilitation groups and approved research (NI2024-486). |
| Last checked | 8 Nov 2026 |

> **Authority's internal categories (for Rowan only, never app copy):** fee items 1.8, 1.9 and 1.15–1.21 (DI2026-133). "Non-exempt animal" is defined by the s 155 declaration (DI2026-61).
